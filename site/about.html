WHERE WE'VE BEEN

We test in the room, not in a spreadsheet.

Three pieces of work that shaped the product. None of them is a commercial agreement, and we describe them as what they were: a pilot, and conversations.

PILOT · KATSINA STATE

A classroom pilot that changed the product

Working with the Katsina State Ministry of Basic and Secondary Education, we ran a prototype pilot with primary and secondary students, testing financial literacy technology in a live classroom.

The finding was uncomfortable and useful: learners found the education interesting but did not want money taught in the abstract. They wanted it applied to their own money. That is the reason Savvy Bee is an intelligence and execution layer attached to real accounts, rather than an advice app.

CONVERSATIONS · FEDERAL MINISTRY

Meetings on the Nigeria for Women Project

We have met with the Federal Ministry of Women Affairs and Social Development to discuss the Nigeria for Women Project and how women’s affinity groups — existing, trusted, community savings groups — might be onboarded as groups rather than as individuals.

These were exploratory conversations. Nothing has been signed, and we are not claiming otherwise. What they gave us was the shape of the Hive: a circle that keeps its own register, schedule and rotation.

CONVERSATIONS · NON-INTEREST BANKING

Sitting down with TAJBank

We have met with TAJBank to discuss non-interest savings and how a Shariah-compliant option would work inside the product — separate capsules, no interest accrual, and a clear label so nobody has to guess.

Also exploratory. It is on the roadmap because a large part of the market we are building for will not use a product that only offers interest-bearing savings.
